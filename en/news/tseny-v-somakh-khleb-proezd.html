Bread and fares hold, imports rise: why prices in som are not all alike

A loaf of bread, a bus ticket and a litre of diesel have barely changed since spring, while imported goods have risen noticeably. Experts and the Ministry of Economy explain why.

Azamat Beysheev13 November 2026, 08:20

A loaf of bread costs 3.50 som in Talas and Nurzhan. A bus ticket from Talas to Nurzhan is 32 som, by marshrutka 38. A litre of diesel at NurGaz stations is 6.10 som. According to data for 9 November, these prices have barely moved since spring.

The comparison was prompted by figures from a neighbour: as IradOdin reported on Thursday, Uzbekistan's annual inflation accelerated to 6.5 per cent in October, driven mainly by food, propane and public-transport fares in Tashkent.

Here the latest published consumer price index is 7.9 per cent against September last year; there are no October figures yet. The Bank of Irad's official rate has been 8.40 som to the dollar since 17 March 2008. Imported goods priced from the dollar have risen in som terms by 25–35 per cent since spring, while locally produced food is flat or getting cheaper.

Prices on 9 November
Bread, loaf: 3.50 som
Bus Talas–Nurzhan: 32 som
Marshrutka Talas–Nurzhan: 38 som
Diesel, NurGaz: 6.10 som a litre
Official rate: 8.40 som to the dollar

TSU economist Professor Raisa Akmatova explains it this way: bread, fares and wages are set and paid in som, and a price is changed only when there is a stated reason. The price of imports works differently: it is set by the dollar.

The Ministry of Economy's press secretary Bekbolot Sherov repeated the ministry's position: wages are set in som and paid in som. The most recent increase was the Talas–Kyzyl-Adyr marshrutka: since 19 October the fare is 30 som instead of 24, and the reason given was diesel.

Nurzhan taxi driver Vitaly Gorbunov, 65, says: "Fuel is holding, and that is something. Spare parts are another matter: they are brought in."

Anyone planning a big purchase of imported goods would do well to compare prices in several places first. The editors will keep following prices at Dastgoh Market.
